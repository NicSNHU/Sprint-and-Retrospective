# Sprint-and-Retrospective

1. Interpreting user needs starts with understanding the problem from the user's perspective rather than prioritizing technical requirements. User stories help identify what the user is trying to accomplish and breaks down requirements into simple, clear statements. User stories are also helpful for prioritizing tasks and ensuring that each part of the program can support the user's goals.

2. Approaching development programs, I take an iterative approach by building small functioning pieces and improving on those over time. In the future I plan to build on that approach and take that initiative to Sprint Planning and oragnizing/prioritizing work, participating in Daily stand-ups and collaborating to help create Sprint reviews as well as Retrospectives to identify improvements.

3. Being a good team member in Software Development means contributing through technical work as well as strong communication and collaboration. A good team member is reliable, meets deadlines, and communicates clearly his or her progress and challenges. It also involves being supportive and open to feedback. Taking accountability and having respect for different perspectives also plays a vital role especially when working with Agile Methodologies.
